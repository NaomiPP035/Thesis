ABOUT THIS ATLAS

A contact can outlast
my attention.

This log follows everyday touch. A new entry comes forward; earlier entries shrink and soften into the background. Select one and it brightens where it is, then fades at the same rate as a new entry.

The movement is a way to explore noticing and no longer noticing. It does not tell us when a real sensation faded, or prove that contact continued.

The September 25–30 source contains reported events, reported habits, inferred scenes and unresolved contacts. A row describes a contact type or a step, rather than a count of actual touches. Broad time labels and unknown dates stay as recorded.

Colors follow the feeling of each entry: coral for pleasure, soft rose for ease, blue for focus, violet for curiosity, burgundy for tiredness, and deeper reds for irritation. Other feelings can use greens, amber and muted neutrals; each family includes lighter, darker and more saturated shades. Close shades belong to related feelings. Where the source did not describe a mood, the feeling is an editable interpretation, not an observed measurement. Brightness and size still follow depth, visual variation and fading attention.
